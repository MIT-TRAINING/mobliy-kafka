Topic 4

Encryption & network security in Confluent-managed environments

A CDR crosses several links: client to broker, broker to broker, broker to controller and to the identity store, and finally the disk. Each one is either encrypted or not, and on Confluent Cloud some aren't yours to configure. Choose a cluster, then tap each link to see what an eavesdropper in that position would capture. Try the course's Apache cluster first. It is plaintext on purpose.

Who encrypts what

LayerApache Kafka / self-managed PlatformConfluent Cloud
Client ↔ brokerYou configure TLS listeners: SSL or SASL_SSL, keystores, truststores, certificate renewalTLS 1.2+ always on; nothing to configure
Broker ↔ broker, controllersYou choose. The inter.broker.listener.name and controller listeners need their own TLS (mTLS on the Platform lab)Run by Confluent
Identity storeLDAP or Kerberos link: use ldaps:// (636)Confluent identities, SSO or OAuth
At restKafka writes plaintext log segments. Encrypt the volume (encrypted EBS on both lab clusters)Always encrypted. Dedicated clusters can use your own key (BYOK)
Network reachPrivate subnets, security groups, advertised.listenersBasic and Standard: public endpoints. Enterprise: PrivateLink. Dedicated: VPC peering, Transit Gateway, PrivateLink
Field-levelSensitive fields such as an MSISDN can be encrypted by the client before producing (Confluent client-side field-level encryption), so brokers and admins never see them
Admin takeaway: TLS on a listener protects the wire, not the disk. Anyone with a shell on a broker, or a copy of an unencrypted volume snapshot, can read /var/lib/kafka/data/l07.cdr.voice-0/*.log with kafka-dump-log.sh. Encrypt volumes, and restrict who can log in to brokers.
Capacity gotcha: with TLS, a broker can no longer use zero-copy sendfile to serve consumers. It has to encrypt every byte in the JVM. Plan for more broker CPU when you enable SASL_SSL on a busy cluster, and measure before and after (Module 8).
