Retention & cleanup policies: delete vs. compact

Retention is judged on the newest record of each segment and deletes whole segments. Advance the clock in part 1 to watch time- and size-based deletion race each other, then in part 2 run all three cleanup policies on the same record set and compare.

The cardinal rule: retention is independent of consumption. Kafka deletes old data whether or not any consumer has read it — a group that is down longer than retention.ms misses data permanently (one of the message-loss scenarios in Module 9). Set retention to exceed your worst-case consumer outage plus margin.

Deletion is not one step: the broker evaluates every log.retention.check.interval.ms (5 min), renames segment files *.deleted, then unlinks them after log.segment.delete.delay.ms (file.delete.delay.ms at topic level, default 60 s). During that gap an extended retention can still rescue the files — which is why a disk often frees up in steps, not smoothly.
