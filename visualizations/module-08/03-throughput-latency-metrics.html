Cluster load metrics: throughput and latency indicators

"Kafka is slow" is not a diagnosis. Every broker splits each request's time into stages: waiting in the request queue, writing locally, waiting for followers, being throttled, and sending the response. The biggest stage names the bottleneck. Push CDR traffic at the l08.cdr.voice leader on cp-broker-1, then break something (a slow disk, a slow follower, a quota) and read which bar grows.

Queue time high

Request handler threads are busy. Find out why: many tiny produce requests (raise linger.ms), a slow disk making each request take longer, or simply too much load for this broker.

Local or remote time high

Local: the leader's disk write is slow, so check iostat and volume metrics. Remote: with acks=all, the leader waits for the slowest in-sync follower.

Throttle time high

Nothing is broken. The broker is delaying responses on purpose because a client is above its quota. On Confluent Cloud the same happens when a cluster reaches its limits (Topic 4).

Admin gotcha: averages hide problems. A produce mean of 4 ms can sit next to a p99 of 400 ms when one follower stalls. Alert and tune on the 99th percentile, per broker, so you see that the slow requests come from one broker.
Production trap: a slow follower first raises acks=all latency. After replica.lag.time.max.ms (30 s by default), the leader drops it from the ISR and latency recovers, so the graph looks fine. But each of those partitions now has one fewer in-sync copy. Watch IsrShrinksPerSec and URP as well as latency.
