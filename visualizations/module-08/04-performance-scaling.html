Performance considerations and scaling in Confluent-managed clusters

CDR traffic is low at night, rises in the morning and peaks around 21:00. Each cluster type handles that peak differently: an elastic Cloud cluster scales itself up to a cap and then throttles, a Dedicated cluster has the capacity you bought, and on Confluent Platform a new broker only helps once partitions move to it. Play the day, then change capacity, compression or the cluster type until the evening peak is handled.

The Cloud figures follow Confluent's published limits: Standard 25 MBps ingress per eCKU, up to 250 MBps per cluster, and Dedicated 60 MBps ingress per CKU. They change over time, so check the cluster-types page for your region. The 80 MB/s per Platform broker is illustrative: size your own brokers from load tests.

Levers that cost nothing

compression.type=lz4 or zstd on the producer shrinks bytes on the network, on disk and in replication. Text-like CDRs compress well. linger.ms and batch.size give larger batches, which compress better and mean fewer requests (Topic 3).

Partitions limit parallelism

Adding capacity doesn't help a consumer group that has run out of partitions. With 6 partitions, a 7th billing-svc instance sits idle. Plan partition counts for the peak consumer count, and stay under the cluster type's partition limit.

Admin takeaway: scale for the peak, not the average. In this simulation the daily average is about a third of the 21:00 peak. Capacity sized on the average is throttled every evening, exactly when billing traffic matters most.
Capacity gotcha: scaling a Dedicated cluster or rebalancing a Platform cluster takes time, because partitions and data have to move. Scale before the peak. Starting an expansion at 20:45 doesn't help the 21:00 peak.
