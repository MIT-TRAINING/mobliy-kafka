SignalRule of thumbWhy

Throughput, disk, connectionsTicket or dashboard, not a pageThese are capacity signals: plan for them during the day (Topic 4)

How Control Center 2.x alerts

Since Confluent Platform 8.0, Control Center is a separate product. On the lab's cp-services node it runs with its own Prometheus (:9090), which stores the brokers' metrics and checks the alert conditions, and Alertmanager (:9093), which sends the notifications. Under Alerts you create a trigger (component type, metric, condition, value and a buffer in seconds) and one or more actions (email, Slack webhook, PagerDuty webhook). A true condition is PENDING until it has held for the buffer, then FIRING. The brokers compute consumer lag themselves (confluent.consumer.lag.emitter.enabled=true), so no client instrumentation is needed.

Reading lag correctly

Lag is log-end offset − committed offset, per partition. 720 000 records sounds alarming, but at 12 000 CDR/s produced and 20 000/s consumed, billing clears it in 720 000 ÷ 8 000 ≈ 90 s. Check whether lag is growing before you compare it with a threshold. Control Center shows lag per partition under the consumer group, so you can also see whether a single partition is stuck.

Admin gotcha: if the trigger is lag > 50k with a 0-second buffer, every rebalance pages you. Within a week the team mutes the channel, and then a real slowdown also goes unnoticed. Set the threshold above normal bursts, add a buffer of a few minutes, and count your pages per week as a health metric of the alerting itself.
Production trap: in the lab, Control Center, its Prometheus and its Alertmanager all run on one node. If cp-services stops, no alert fires, so the dashboard just shows no problems. Watch the watcher: add an outside check (an uptime probe of :9021, or an always-firing heartbeat alert that pages you when it stops arriving).
