Topic 1 · Producer configuration tuning

send() is not a network call

Serialize. Key and value become bytes. The broker never sees your Java object.

Partition. Same key, same partition, so each MSISDN stays in order. No key: the sticky partitioner fills one batch at a time.

Accumulate. Records wait in per-partition batches inside buffer.memory, 32 MB by default.

Send. A background thread ships batches to the leaders. The callback gets the offset or the exception.

Point at the two threads: your code on the left, the sender on the right. send() only serializes, picks a partition and drops the record into a batch; the network call happens later. That is why an app that exits without close() or flush() loses whatever is still in the accumulator. Ask: if the broker rejects the batch, where does your code find out?

Raising linger.ms from 5 to 20 ms usually costs a few milliseconds and buys fuller batches, better compression, and fewer requests on every broker, so change one knob at a time and measure. retries defaults to 2147483647, so the real limit is delivery.timeout.ms, two minutes by default. It covers the linger, every attempt, and the backoffs in between. Retriable errors such as NOT_LEADER_OR_FOLLOWER during a leader election are handled inside the client; your callback only sees the final outcome. Topic 1 · Producer configuration tuning · idempotence

Idempotence turns a retry into a harmless repeat

On by default since Kafka 3.0

enable.idempotence=true together with acks=all. You get it for free as long as you don't turn it off.

An explicit acks=1 turns it off

Idempotence needs acks=all, retries, and at most 5 in-flight requests. If your settings conflict, the default quietly switches off.

Order is kept

Up to 5 in-flight batches per connection still land in order, because the broker rejects any gap in the sequence.

One producer session only

A restarted producer gets a new ID. Deduplicating across restarts, or over a read-process-write step, needs transactions.

The ack was lost, not the write, so a plain retry duplicates the CDR. With idempotence, the broker tracks a producer ID and a sequence number for the last five batches on each partition, and it answers a repeat with an ack instead of a second write. Look for acks=1 in old config files: it silently switches the default off. Ask: does idempotence protect you if your own code catches the exception and calls send() again?
Topic 2 · Consumer groups

Inside a group, each partition has one owner

!
MQ admins: there are no competing consumers on one partition. A group can use at most as many consumers as the topic has partitions, and any extra consumer sits idle. Each group keeps its own offsets, so adding a group never takes records away from billing.
group.id is the whole identity: same group means the partitions are shared out, a different group means another full copy of the stream. The group coordinator is one broker, and it stores each group's committed offsets in the internal __consumer_offsets topic; as you saw in Module 2, adding partitions later remaps keys, so size for the parallelism you will need. Ask: billing runs 8 instances on a 6-partition topic. What do the extra two do?
Topic 2 · Offset management and commit strategies

The committed offset is a bookmark, not a delete

StrategyHow it worksThe catch
auto commit (default)enable.auto.commit=true. poll() commits every auto.commit.interval.ms, 5 s by defaultDuplicates after a crash. Records are lost if you hand them to another thread.
commitSync()Auto commit off. Commit after the whole batch is processedAt-least-once. Blocks until the coordinator answers.
commitAsync()Fast commit in the loop, then commitSync() on close or revokeA failed async commit is not retried.
auto.offset.resetlatest (default), earliest, or noneOnly applies when the group has no committed offset yet.
Consuming a record does not remove it; retention does, on the Module 3 schedule. The committed offset is the next offset the group will read, stored in __consumer_offsets, and lag is the log end minus that number. Whatever you polled but did not commit is read again after a crash, so design the processing to tolerate a repeat. Ask: with auto commit on, which records are at risk if the process is killed?

Lag is the gap between LOG-END-OFFSET and CURRENT-OFFSET, per partition. One partition lagging while the rest sit at zero usually means one slow consumer or one hot key. A reset rewrites the committed offset, so it only works while the group has no active members, and you always run --dry-run before --execute. Topic 2 · Rebalancing

Every rebalance is a pause. Keep it small.

Heartbeats stop

No heartbeat for session.timeout.ms, 45 s by default, and the member is removed. That is usually a crash or a network break.

poll() comes too late

More than max.poll.interval.ms, 5 min, between polls and the member is removed. The late commit then fails. Lower max.poll.records (500).

Static membership

Set group.instance.id per instance. A restart that comes back within the session timeout triggers no rebalance.

New protocol in Kafka 4.0

group.protocol=consumer (KIP-848) lets the broker assign partitions incrementally. The client default is still classic.

A rebalance happens whenever group membership or the topic's partitions change: a deploy, a crash, a scale-out; with eager assignment everybody drops everything first, while the cooperative-sticky assignor moves only what must move. The most common production cause is slow processing: the loop misses max.poll.interval.ms, the member is removed, and its commit fails. Ask: a rolling deploy of 6 billing pods triggers 12 rebalances. What would you set?
Topic 3 · Delivery semantics

Where you commit decides lost, twice, or once

SemanticsProducer sideConsumer sideTelecom fit
at-most-onceacks=0 or 1, no retriesCommit, then processLive dashboards, sampled network metrics
at-least-onceacks=all + idempotence (the defaults)Process, then commit. Deduplicate in the sink by key.CDR billing, the everyday choice
exactly-oncetransactional.id, commitTransaction()isolation.level=read_committedKafka-to-Kafka steps: rating → billing topic
Exactly-once in Kafka covers read-process-write inside Kafka: the output records and the input offsets commit atomically, and read_committed consumers never see an aborted transaction. Once you write to a database or call an API, you are back to at-least-once plus an idempotent write, for example an upsert keyed by the CDR ID. The shared cluster's ACLs cover your topic and group prefixes; if a transactional.id is refused there, run the transaction demo on your Module 2 cluster. Topic 4 · Writing Kafka producer and consumer applications in Java

One producer per app, one consumer per thread

// both apps: SASL settings from the lab VM, then the endpoint Properties p = new Properties(); p.load(Files.newInputStream(Path.of(HOME, "kafka/apache.properties"))); p.put("bootstrap.servers", "apache-kafka.lab.internal:9092"); // ---- CdrProducer ---- p.put("key.serializer", StringSerializer.class.getName()); p.put("value.serializer", StringSerializer.class.getName()); p.put("linger.ms", "20"); p.put("compression.type", "lz4"); try (var producer = new KafkaProducer<String, String>(p)) { producer.send(new ProducerRecord<>(ME + ".cdr.voice", msisdn, json), (meta, ex) -> { if (ex != null) log.error("not sent", ex); }); } // close() flushes open batches // ---- BillingConsumer ---- (deserializers instead) p.put("group.id", ME + ".billing"); p.put("enable.auto.commit", "false"); p.put("auto.offset.reset", "earliest"); try (var consumer = new KafkaConsumer<String, String>(p)) { consumer.subscribe(List.of(ME + ".cdr.voice")); while (running) { for (var r : consumer.poll(Duration.ofMillis(500))) bill(r); consumer.commitSync(); // after the batch: at-least-once } }
The producer is thread-safe and expensive to create, so build one per application and share it. The consumer is not thread-safe, so run one per thread and scale by adding instances up to the partition count. Loading apache.properties keeps credentials out of the code, and for a clean shutdown, call consumer.wakeup() from a shutdown hook so the loop exits and close() leaves the group straight away. Ask: what happens to lag if bill() takes 2 seconds and max.poll.records is 500?
Topic 5 · Error handling and retry patterns

Let the client retry transient errors. Park the poison.

No resend loop around send()

The client already retries. Your resend is a new record with a new sequence number, so idempotence cannot catch the duplicate.

Never retry a record forever

An endless retry blocks the partition. The poll gap then passes max.poll.interval.ms and triggers a rebalance.

Dead-letter topic

Publish the failed record to cdr.voice.DLT with the error in its headers, commit, and replay it after the fix.

Frameworks do this for you

In Spring Kafka, DefaultErrorHandler with DeadLetterPublishingRecoverer gives you retry, backoff and a DLT.

On the producer side, sort errors into two buckets: retriable ones, such as a leader move or NOT_ENOUGH_REPLICAS, are the client's job. Non-retriable ones, such as RecordTooLargeException, a serialization error or a missing ACL, reach your callback and need a human. On the consumer side the pattern is retry a little, then dead-letter, so one bad CDR never stalls the whole partition. Ask: who owns the DLT, and who is paged when it grows?

Wrap-up

Key takeaways, and a quick self-check

01send() is asynchronous

Batches leave at batch.size or linger.ms. Read the callback and close the producer.

02Time bounds retries

delivery.timeout.ms, 2 min by default, ends retrying, not the retry count.

03Keep idempotence on

It is the default with acks=all. Don't set acks=1 and don't write a resend loop.

04One owner per partition

A group's parallelism stops at the partition count. Each group has its own offsets.

05Commit after processing

At-least-once with an idempotent sink is the workhorse. Use transactions for Kafka-to-Kafka steps.

06Keep the loop moving

A slow poll loop triggers rebalances. Park poison records in a DLT.

click to revealA topic has 6 partitions and group billing runs 8 consumers. What do the extra two do?
They sit idle as standbys. To use more than 6 consumers, you need more partitions.
click to revealYou set acks=1 "for speed". What else changed without telling you?
Idempotence switched off, so retries can now write duplicates. You can also lose writes if the leader fails.
click to revealThe consumer commits before processing, then crashes mid-batch. Which semantics is that?
At-most-once. The uncommitted work is skipped for good on restart.
click to reveal--reset-offsets --execute fails and says the group is not inactive. Why?
Consumers in the group are still running. Stop every member, then run the reset again.
